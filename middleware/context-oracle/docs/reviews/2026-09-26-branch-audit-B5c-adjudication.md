### E-4
**Ruling:** Both reviews say `replace`; the verdict stands. Both reasoning disputes are ruled for the second opinion after reproduction, and the root-cause question put to this adjudication is answered: the `h ≥ 37` floor plus a `ts` clamp is not the root-cause design; re-basing the epoch is. (1) The `ts` hole's backing. The first audit cites AD-13 L1458–L1459 for "whatever the committer's clock said"; that line says only that `git log` is streamed. The real backing is that the plan's one stream reads `%at`, git's author date as a UNIX timestamp, which any commit may set (`git commit --date`, `GIT_AUTHOR_DATE`) — verified against git's documentation below. (2) The re-base alternative. The first audit's "re-base the epoch on a full mine would cap the exponent by the horizon (5 years / `h`) and need no `tune` floor" is wrong: incremental passes after the full mine add commits with later `ts`, so `(ts − T_base)/h` grows with wall-clock time since the full mine and has no horizon cap without a forced re-base. The alternative the arithmetic actually supports is the one AD-13's own premise makes exact: every stored weight shares the factor `2^(−Δ/h)` for any shift Δ of the epoch, so multiplying every `pair_weight` and `change_weight` by that factor re-bases the store in one `UPDATE` with no re-mine, and if Δ is a whole number of half-lives the factor is a power of two and the multiplication is exact in binary64. Reproduced (`rebase.mjs`): at `h` = 20, below the floor, the ratio is identical before and after re-basing to 2026-09-28 and the rescaled stored sum equals the re-based sum. With the epoch kept near `refTs` the exponent of every in-horizon commit lies in a fixed band that does not move with the calendar, so no floor on `h` follows from overflow; the only floor the mechanism itself needs is from underflow of the oldest in-horizon term (5 years / `h` ≤ 1022 for a normal double: `h` > 1.79 days). By contrast the hunk's design keeps the cause — a fixed 2000 epoch whose exponent grows with calendar time — and bounds one input: at the floor `h` = 37 the exponent passes 1000 on 2101-04-21 and 1024 on 2103-09-26, and it presumes `ts` is real-world (a 2110 commit is `Infinity`, the ratio `NaN`, `c ≥ floor` silently false — B5b H1 E-15's settled hole). A `ts ≤ refTs` cap is needed in every design, because a commit dated after `refTs` dominates every ratio it enters on any `h` (B5b H1 E-15); it does not by itself make the fixed epoch safe, since `refTs` is `HEAD`'s own date. The arithmetic the hunk records is correct (reproduced), "1000" is 1024 less unstated headroom, and the day unit closes B5a E-19's ambiguity; the decision between the two fixes §16 (b) offered is not recorded, and the fixed epoch's cost (a floor on FR-K2's tunable that exists only because of the epoch) is not stated — the brief's test 5 fails. Context only, not ruled here: the branch's later Step 13 review commit `c31d87e` re-based the epoch per full mine, and its own "Why" names "a fixed 2000 epoch" as what overflowed; that commit is judged in its own batch.
