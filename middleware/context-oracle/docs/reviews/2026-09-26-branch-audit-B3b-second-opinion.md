### E-7
**Agree/Disagree:** Agree with the verdict (`replace`). The reasoning holds in substance but one step overreaches. Re-run: the first auditor's harness models the architecture's event path faithfully (100 ms `busy_timeout`, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, one immediate retry — AD-26 states no backoff) and models a conforming off-path pass: AD-26 lets `git log` be aggregated outside the transaction and then commits chunks of 50 ms of writing, with no gap required, so writing all chunks back to back is permitted by the text. It is harsher than a real pass in one respect only — the pass lasts 4 s and writes ~3.6 M upserts, where the reviewer's 10,000-commit write phase was 414 ms — which lengthens the exposure window but does not change the per-event outcome while the pass is writing. My re-run: at 50 ms chunks and no gap, 14 of 14 handler runs that overlapped the pass failed (the one success came after the miner exited: `ok:true, attempts:2`, printed after the miner's summary). So the finding holds for the design as written: chunk length is not what bounds the handler's wait; the gap is. Where I part from the first audit is the fix's size: its Alternatives say yield "for at least the event path's full wait (~200 ms)". That is not backed and is costly (80 chunks × 200 ms adds 16 s to a 4 s pass). SQLite's default busy handler sleeps at most 25 ms between polls within a 100 ms timeout, so a yield at least that long guarantees one poll lands in a free window; executed, a 5 ms gap already gave 15/15 and 2 ms gave 8/15. The verdict line (an inter-chunk yield that lets the event path in, pinned by a fixture) is right; its size should be derived from the busy handler's poll schedule, not from the full wait. The `mining_in_progress` visibility point is correct: `status`'s suppressing-conditions list omits it.

### E-15
