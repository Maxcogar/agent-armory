### E-4
**Agree/Disagree:** Verdict — agree (`replace`). Reasoning — agree on the arithmetic, on the `ts` side of the bound and on the unexplained 1000. I disagree on two supporting points.
- **The arithmetic reproduces exactly.** Day 36,525 is 2100-01-01. `2^(36525/30)` is `Infinity`. `2^(36525/37)` is 1.46e297 ("about 1.5e297"). The last day of 2100 (day 36,889) at `h` = 37 gives 1.34e300. 10,000 such terms are about 1.46e301, under `2^1024`. At `h` = 37 the exponent reaches 1000 on 2101-04-21 and 1024 on 2103-09-26, so "before 2100" is a conservative subset of `(ts − T0)/h < 1000`, and the 37-day floor is correct for its stated range. A 2110 commit at `h` = 37 gives `Infinity` and a ratio of `NaN`, which is the unbounded-`ts` hole that B5b H1 E-15 settled.
- **First disagreement: the evidence for the `ts` hole.** The first audit's claim that the date is "whatever the committer's clock said" is cited to AD-13 L1458–L1459, which says only that `git log` is streamed. That line does not back the claim. The real backing is that the plan reads the **author** date (`%at`), and git lets any commit set it (`--date`, `GIT_AUTHOR_DATE`).
- **Second disagreement: alternative (b), re-basing the epoch.** The first audit says re-basing "on a full mine" would "cap the exponent by the horizon (5 years / `h`)" and need "no `tune` floor". It would not. Incremental passes keep adding commits after the base, so `ts − T_base` grows with time since the last full mine and has no horizon cap. The alternative the numbers support needs no re-mine at all. Every stored weight shares the factor `2^(−T/h)`, the same premise AD-13 relies on at L1479–L1481. So multiplying every `pair_weight` and `change_weight` by `2^(−Δ/h)` re-bases the store exactly, in one update, at any time (reproduced: the same ratio at `h` = 20, below the floor, before and after re-basing). That is the same exact rescaling B4 settled as valid ("rescaling at read"). This makes the missing comparison sharper, not weaker. The fixed epoch buys simplicity, and it costs a floor on a tunable (FR-K2's "configurable recency-weighted horizon") that exists only because of that choice. The hunk records neither side of that trade.
